# Equipment Foundation Research 01

**Date:** 2026-10-16

## Research question

How should Rookvale structure equipment rarity, tiering, Unique items, drop density, baseline gearing, and set-item policy without becoming a loot-flood ARPG or starving players of usable gear?

## Sources reviewed

### Last Epoch

Useful observations:

- rarity is strongly tied to affix structure;
- Common has no affixes, Magic has 1-2, Rare has 3-4;
- Unique items contain effects unavailable on ordinary lower-rarity items;
- Exalted items contain drop-only high-tier affixes;
- the game distinguishes Unique identity from ordinary affix crafting;
- official 2026 set-item guidance explicitly notes that large sets consume many gear slots, reduce customization, and can make players feel like they are choosing a predefined kit rather than building a character;
- newer set direction trends toward smaller two-piece sets.

Rookvale synthesis:

- separate item rarity from progression Tier;
- keep affix counts bounded;
- avoid general multi-piece set progression;
- let Unique items change rules/build direction rather than only inflate stats.

### Grim Dawn

Useful observations:

- loot is intended to remain valuable while still being obtainable without endless trading/grinding;
- exploration chests and one-time chests can guarantee meaningful rewards;
- Monster Infrequents give specific monsters target-farm identity;
- Epic/Legendary equipment uses predetermined or highly distinctive effects;
- bosses can have exclusive drops;
- equipment rewards coexist with exploration rewards instead of relying only on global random tables;
- set completion can become difficult to give up due to strong combined bonuses.

Rookvale synthesis:

- targetable boss/monster/dungeon loot should matter;
- guaranteed progression rewards can protect against equipment starvation;
- traditional multi-piece sets are an unnecessary build-lock risk.

### Monster Hunter

Useful observations:

- equipment can be forged through materials and currency instead of depending solely on direct equipment drops;
- weapon/armor upgrading gives players a deterministic way to maintain functional equipment;
- quest/gathering/material rewards keep reward screens meaningful even when complete weapons do not drop constantly.

Rookvale synthesis:

- fewer complete-equipment drops do not imply empty rewards;
- current-Tier baseline equipment should have deterministic paths through shops/crafting/quests as well as drops.

## Design conclusions

1. Equipment Tier and Rarity are separate axes.
2. Character Tier controls the broad equipment generation the player can legitimately use/obtain.
3. Low-Tier Unique items do not automatically ascend to later-Tier-equivalent power.
4. Later-Tier Unique items need their own stronger identity and reason to pursue new content.
5. Traditional 4-6 piece set systems are excluded from the default design.
6. Ordinary randomized affixes remain limited and readable.
7. Unique equipment should create build opportunities and mechanical rule changes.
8. Equipment drop density can remain moderate because combat also rewards XP, Mastery XP, gold, materials, monster parts, consumables, vendor items, collectibles, and cosmetics.
9. Players should not enter a legitimate new Character Tier and remain severely undergeared due to bad RNG.
10. Every Tier therefore needs dependable baseline gear sources: progression quests, shops, crafting, first clears, ordinary drops, or similar paths.
11. High-quality optimized items remain rarer than baseline gear.
12. Important gear should be reasonably targetable through identifiable content rather than existing only in one giant global random table.

## Remaining research

Future passes should separately study:

- affix taxonomy and stat budgets;
- crafting/enhancement failure and cost structures;
- Unique duplicate handling and drop protection;
- trade/bind systems;
- transmog/cosmetic preservation of obsolete equipment;
- merchant progression and catch-up rules.
