# Rookvale Starting Region Layout Draft 01

**Status:** Draft. Candidate B is the preferred topology after research/audit, but exact geometry, names, enemy species, dungeon fiction, distances, and encounter counts are not yet Locked.

## Goal

Create a first Region that teaches Rookvale's exploration grammar without becoming either:

- an obvious three-spoke tutorial hub;
- or a deliberately obscure labyrinth.

The player should understand the ordinary direction of play, while still choosing to leave the obvious road for discovery.

## Preferred topology: asymmetric valley loops

The starting Region is a river valley with one historically important north-south route and one principal crossing near the central settlement.

The settlement is **not geometrically centered** in the Region. It exists where the road meets a useful crossing and surrounding farmland/traffic can support it.

The river and elevation create natural asymmetry.

## Broad player-facing read

When a new player leaves the central settlement:

- **south** should visibly read as the safest ordinary countryside and first-learning space;
- **north** should read as the important but increasingly damaged historic route and the broad direction of major progression;
- **west** should visibly read as optional exploratory terrain that still reconnects to useful routes;
- **east across the main bridge** should be understandable as a smaller optional pocket with conspicuous danger farther out.

The player should not need to memorize four equally important exits at once.

The main road hierarchy does most of the orientation work.

## Settlement placement

The central settlement sits near the principal crossing, slightly west/southwest of the bridge rather than being symmetrically wrapped around the river.

Reasons:

- road traffic historically converged on the crossing;
- the settlement can extend toward relatively dry ground and farmland;
- the bridge remains a strong visual/navigation anchor;
- the town naturally has an older road-facing core and later outskirts;
- the map avoids becoming a symmetrical twin-bank city too early.

The settlement should have multiple internal districts, but the vertical slice only needs enough of them to communicate history and service identity.

## South farmland: low-risk learning space

Purpose:

- teach ordinary exploration without making the player feel trapped in a tutorial corridor;
- show that the world is inhabited and economically used;
- establish the difference between maintained territory and abandoned territory;
- give the player a safe route for initial quests, gathering, NPC encounters, and basic combat.

Layout rules:

- broad readable road;
- hedges, drainage channels, field boundaries, minor elevation, barns/sheds, and river access create small tactical variations;
- at least one short optional loop returns to the main road rather than ending in a long dead end;
- one non-combat discovery is placed early so the player learns that exploration does not always mean another fight.

Enemy role draft:

- small crop/food scavengers;
- burrowing or nest-based creatures near neglected field edges;
- river-margin predator only near water;
- rare aggressive encounter near an abandoned parcel, not every field.

Maintained farms and the road itself remain relatively quiet.

## West woodland/upland: curiosity route

Purpose:

- make optional exploration materially worthwhile;
- demonstrate loop/rejoin design;
- introduce more dangerous enemy behavior without becoming the main progression gate;
- host the first Field Boss and first Small Dungeon.

The western route begins close enough to town that players can see it as a legitimate option, then becomes less maintained as it climbs into woodland/upland terrain.

### Field Boss territory

The Field Boss does not stand in the only path forward.

Its territory is foreshadowed through several of:

- damaged livestock fencing;
- tracks;
- carcass remains;
- warning behavior from ordinary creatures;
- missing-animal talk in nearby NPC dialogue;
- visible movement/silhouette;
- changes in ambient sound.

A player can retreat before committing.

The boss species and final fiction remain separate design work.

### Small Dungeon

The first Small Dungeon should not be a random cave inserted because the map needs a dungeon icon.

Preferred world-function candidates:

- abandoned quarry;
- old service/drainage tunnel;
- former extraction tunnel;
- cellar/storage network attached to an abandoned work site.

Its most useful structural role is to connect or reveal a relationship between the west route and the northern route.

Possible reward outcomes:

- second exit creating a shortcut;
- blocked deeper branch for later return;
- evidence explaining why monsters reached another Area;
- access to a useful overlook/route anchor.

Exact dungeon type remains Draft until local geology/economy is finalized.

## North road: first escalation corridor

Purpose:

- establish that Rookvale is not a safe pastoral world;
- show the decline of once-maintained infrastructure through play rather than exposition;
- lead naturally toward the first Major Dungeon and the later northward world route.

The road starts legible and historically important, then becomes progressively less maintained.

Environmental sequence may include combinations of:

- neglected milestones/signage;
- closed roadside businesses;
- damaged patrol infrastructure;
- abandoned carts or cargo;
- narrowed road sections;
- overgrowth;
- improvised barricades;
- ruined checkpoint structures.

Enemy role draft:

- fewer but more authored encounters than a filler-heavy gauntlet;
- intelligent or semi-intelligent occupiers can use ruined walls, elevation, barricades, or support roles;
- patrols can cross the road instead of every enemy waiting statically in the player's lane;
- encounter composition changes as the player approaches the Major Dungeon.

## First Major Dungeon

The first Major Dungeon sits where the northern valley/road narrows enough that a fortification or controlled route facility makes historical sense.

First-read fiction should be simple:

> this place once controlled/protected an important route and is now dangerous because of its current occupants.

Deeper history can reveal later construction layers, older foundations, or an unexpected substructure without forcing a lore lecture.

Dungeon-design requirements:

- physically visible/foreshadowed from the northern approach;
- its position explains why the north route is currently disrupted;
- enemy use of guardrooms, walls, courtyards, storage, gates, or vertical structure should reflect occupation of an existing place;
- boss arena should reveal what the space originally was or how it has been repurposed;
- a sealed/later branch may eventually connect to deeper world infrastructure, but this is not mandatory for first implementation.

Clearing the dungeon may eventually reopen or stabilize northward movement, but exact progression consequences remain Draft.

## East floodplain: visible optional danger

Purpose:

- teach fixed authored danger early;
- provide an area the player remembers for later;
- make the main bridge meaningful without turning east into another equal-sized tutorial branch.

The east bank is smaller in the initial slice.

Near the bridge it remains readable and relatively safe. Farther out, terrain/infrastructure should communicate abandonment or instability.

One **over-Tier site or creature** is placed where the player can see/recognize it before accidental commitment.

Rules:

- it does not block the main progression road;
- it is not hidden behind an unavoidable surprise attack;
- escaping is straightforward;
- later return should be rewarding enough that players remember the early sighting.

Exact monster/site remains Draft.

## Quiet-space requirement

The Region intentionally contains quiet movement/screens.

The first slice should not follow a rhythm like:

```text
combat -> 15 seconds walking -> combat -> 15 seconds walking -> combat
```

Quiet intervals can contain:

- scenery;
- NPC traffic;
- environmental evidence;
- visible distant danger;
- a fork in the road;
- farming/industry activity;
- sound cues;
- a landmark;
- an unoccupied shelter;
- a clue toward a future site.

## Encounter-placement matrix

| Sub-area | Ordinary threat | Main encounter identity | Road safety | Major optional threat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Settlement | none | social/service | safe | none |
| South farmland | low | scavenger/burrower/river-edge ecology | high | small local spike only |
| West woodland/upland | low-medium | hunting packs/territorial encounters | medium | Field Boss |
| North old road | medium, rising | patrol/occupation/chokepoint tactics | falling northward | Major Dungeon approach |
| East floodplain | low near bridge, high pocket | sparse ecology + warning space | medium | visible over-Tier site |
| Small Dungeon | focused | tunnel/site-specific composition | n/a | miniboss possible |
| Major Dungeon | high for current band | occupation + authored dungeon roles | n/a | dungeon boss |

## Navigation clarity design

### Default world cues

The player should understand the basic geography through combinations of:

- visibly different road widths/materials;
- bridge/gate silhouettes;
- signposts/milestones;
- settlement traffic;
- fence/field orientation;
- river channel;
- background/large sprites where possible;
- smoke/lights/activity;
- map/journal labels for already-known major destinations.

### Main objective presentation

The default experience should avoid a permanent glowing line across every screen.

However, Rookvale will not make basic objective navigation a purity test.

If the player wants help or a specific quest is spatially ambiguous, candidate support includes:

- area-level objective marking;
- direction/distance indicator;
- optional navigation accessibility setting;
- clearer NPC route instructions;
- highlighting the relevant known road on the map.

Secrets and rare content are not automatically exposed by the same system.

## Candidate comparison audit

Ratings below are internal planning judgments, not player-facing scores.

| Criterion | A: hub/spokes | B: valley loops | C: split-bank network |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| first-time navigation clarity | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| natural settlement/road logic | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| useful detour/rejoin potential | 2 | 5 | 5 |
| enemy-ecology fit | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| dungeon placement logic | 4 | 5 | 5 |
| later-return potential | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| 2D readability | 5 | 4 | 2 |

| initial implementation cost | low | medium | high |

Candidate B is preferred because it has no critical weakness and preserves the strongest advantages of the other two.

## First implementation cut

The implemented vertical slice should **not** build every eventual route in the full Region.

Recommended first playable cut:

- enough of central settlement to support services/NPC flow;
- south farmland Area;
- first western woodland/upland Area;
- short northern old-road section;
- Small Dungeon;
- Field Boss territory;
- visible but mostly unimplemented east-bank over-Tier site;
- Major Dungeon approach and, when ready, the Major Dungeon itself.

Future exits/loops can be blocked by believable temporary or permanent geography during the slice as long as the final graph is reserved in the plan.

## Required next audit before locking geometry

1. Produce 2-3 actual greybox diagrams using this topology with different river bends/elevation distributions.
2. Trace a first-time critical path through each diagram.
3. Trace at least four optional detours and measure whether rejoining becomes annoying.
4. Verify that the Field Boss can be noticed and avoided.
5. Verify that the over-Tier east site cannot be mistaken for mandatory progression.
6. Verify that dungeon entrances are visible/discoverable at the intended level of secrecy.
7. Place quiet intervals before final encounter counts.
8. Re-run the map against the external reference checklist.
9. Do not lock final proper names until the local naming family is designed.
10. When implemented, revise from playtest evidence rather than defending the paper layout.
