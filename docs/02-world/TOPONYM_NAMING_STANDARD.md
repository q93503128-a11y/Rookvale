# Toponym and Place-Naming Standard

**Status:** Core naming direction locked; actual cultural lexicons and final place names remain Draft

## Core direction

Rookvale does not default to transparent English fantasy compounds.

Names such as `Shadow + Wood`, `Iron + Keep`, `Frost + Fang`, `Ember + Reach`, or similar constructions are not a general naming template.

Most frequently used place names should read first as **proper nouns**, not as immediately decomposable gameplay labels.

This mirrors the everyday experience of real place names: speakers often recognize the name without consciously knowing its original historical meaning.

## Preferred name categories

Rookvale may use a mixture of:

### Opaque inherited names

Old names whose original meaning is no longer obvious to modern speakers.

These are often preferred for major towns, rivers, valleys, Regions, and old settlements.

### Phonetically changed historical names

A place may begin from an older word/name and change through centuries of pronunciation, spelling, conquest, migration, or administrative standardization.

The final modern form does not need to transparently reveal the source.

### Personal-origin names

Places may derive from:

- founders;
- old landowners;
- clans/families;
- saints/heroes;
- rulers;
- explorers;
- notorious inhabitants.

The modern player may not know this unless they investigate.

### Borrowed names

A newer population may retain or alter the name used by an older population.

The name may survive long after the language that produced it has disappeared locally.

### Object/event-derived names

A settlement or landmark may have been named after an animal, tool, ship, tree, event, banner, relic, local object, or unusual observation by its founders.

Over time the connection may be forgotten or become folklore.

### Transparent descriptive names

Some names may still have obvious meaning when the history makes that plausible.

Examples in principle:

- a recently established mining camp named for a visible feature;
- a military outpost with a practical descriptive title;
- a nickname that replaced an older official name;
- a sailor/explorer naming a newly charted island after something seen there.

Transparent names are allowed; they are simply not the default pattern for the whole world.

## One-word preference

For many important locations, a short one-word proper noun is preferred over a two-noun fantasy compound when both would work equally well.

This does not mean names must be meaningless internally. The design team may know the etymology even when the player does not.

A good name can therefore have:

- an internal historical derivation;
- a modern opaque surface form;
- optional discoverable etymology.

## Cultural consistency

Opaque names are not random syllables.

Each linguistic/cultural region still receives phonological and historical constraints:

- likely consonant/vowel patterns;
- usual word length;
- historical sound changes;
- old versus modern forms;
- borrowing patterns;
- personal-name conventions;
- common endings that arise naturally from that language history.

The goal is `meaning not immediately transparent`, not `meaningless noise`.

## Layering and alternate names

The same place may have:

- an old local name;
- a modern official name;
- a shortened everyday form;
- a neighboring culture's exonym;
- a military/administrative label;
- a nickname.

Use this only where it adds believable history or useful characterization; do not burden every minor cave with three names.

## Naming workflow

Before final naming of a Region:

1. research several real historical naming systems relevant to the intended cultural texture;
2. define the local phonological/name rules;
3. define historical population/language layers;
4. determine what kind of thing is being named and who named it;
5. generate multiple candidates;
6. reject candidates that sound like generic fantasy compound generators;
7. check pronunciation, memorability, visual readability, and similarity to existing Rookvale names;
8. verify that neighboring names do not all share the same artificial cadence;
9. only then lock the final name.

## Readability

Names used constantly by players should usually be:

- pronounceable after seeing them once or twice;
- short or moderate in length;
- visually distinguishable from other nearby names;
- not overloaded with punctuation;
- not composed of arbitrary apostrophes or diacritics solely to signal fantasy.

Long ceremonial names may exist for institutions or historical documents, but ordinary speech can use shorter forms.

## Rookvale

`Rookvale` remains established as the project/start-Region name.

It does not force the entire surrounding naming family to use `-vale`, English compounds, bird names, or the same visible construction.

The rest of the Region's names will be developed through this standard rather than derived mechanically from `Rookvale`.
