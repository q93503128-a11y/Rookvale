# World Macro Regions

**Status:** Initial macro-world shape locked as a planning target; all names except `Rookvale`, exact borders, culture names, and final Tier ranges remain Draft

## Long-term initial Lv80 world target

Rookvale's first complete long-form world is planned around:

- **8 principal surface Regions** on one main landmass;
- **Outer Isles** as a dispersed sea/island macro-region;
- **Deepways** as a connected underground macro-region that intersects several surface Regions.

This is a design target, not a promise that all ten macro-regions are implemented before the first playable release.

The first production slice remains intentionally much smaller.

## Why one main landmass first

A single large landmass with layered cultures and travel networks is preferred over several thinly sketched continents because it supports:

- repeated historical contact between cultures;
- believable road/trade networks;
- revisiting old areas through newly opened routes;
- overlapping political borders;
- visible consequences of changing trade;
- stronger production density;
- easier web/Roblox/mobile partitioning.

Future expansions may add distant lands when there is enough content to justify them.

## Macro layout philosophy

The world is **not** a radial wheel with Rookvale at the geometric center.

Regions connect through loops, alternate routes, passes, coasts, river corridors, ferries, and Deepways links.

The player should sometimes be able to route around a difficult border or blocked road rather than follow one mandatory chain.

## Starting Region — Rookvale

`Rookvale` is the established starting Region name and the game's title anchor.

Draft identity:

- inland valley and crossroads;
- river and old road network;
- agricultural land, woodland, low hills, and older fortifications;
- historically important because people and goods moved **through** it;
- no longer as busy as it once was;
- some decline is recent enough to exist in living memory;
- older structures reveal several earlier political/historical layers;
- ordinary low-Tier routes coexist with dangerous places intended for much later return.

Rookvale should visually communicate that its current quietness is not its only historical state.

## Surface Region roles

The following are **working functional labels**, not final in-world names.

### Surface Region 1 — Rookvale

Broad progression emphasis: early game, roughly Tier I-II with later-danger pockets.

Functions:

- starting settlement and first multi-route exploration;
- introduces the long-retreat historical theme;
- old road/river infrastructure;
- first visible later-return locations;
- first link into Deepways;
- several routes toward neighboring regions.

### Surface Region 2 — Western woodland/agricultural march

Broad progression emphasis: Tier I-III overlap.

Functions:

- older rural settlements and hunting/forestry economy;
- border changes preserved in ruined defenses and place names;
- lowland woodland, fields, streams, old estates, and abandoned frontier works;
- demonstrates that a Region can contain both settled country and wilderness.

### Surface Region 3 — Highland/mining belt

Broad progression emphasis: Tier II-IV overlap.

Functions:

- mountains, passes, mining settlements, quarrying, fortified transport routes;
- settlements shaped by ore, water, and pass control;
- deeper underground connections;
- visible conflict between old infrastructure and newer extraction.

### Surface Region 4 — Southern coast and wetlands

Broad progression emphasis: Tier II-IV overlap.

Functions:

- ports, marshes, fishing, shipping, coastal cliffs, river mouths, islands close to shore;
- demonstrates trade routes that helped bypass older inland centers such as Rookvale;
- access point toward Outer Isles;
- smuggling, maritime commerce, and changing coastal wealth can create local conflicts.

### Surface Region 5 — central metropolitan/crownland belt

Broad progression emphasis: Tier III-V overlap.

Functions:

- largest urban concentration;
- administration, religion, guilds, long-distance trade, political institutions;
- fertile surrounding countryside rather than an isolated city tile;
- multiple historical building layers and competing narratives about central authority.

This Region must not become the only place where important services exist.

### Surface Region 6 — eastern river/wildland basin

Broad progression emphasis: Tier III-VI overlap.

Functions:

- large rivers, wetlands/forests, unusual ecology, old sacred/communal sites;
- settlement patterns less centered on the road institutions dominant farther west;
- demonstrates a culture that interprets old infrastructure/history differently;
- contains deep dangerous interior pockets far above entry difficulty.

### Surface Region 7 — northern highland/cold reach

Broad progression emphasis: Tier V-VII overlap.

Functions:

- cold highlands, forests, lakes, wind-exposed plateaus, passes, pilgrimage/travel routes;
- varied microclimates rather than one endless snow biome;
- old remote installations and difficult long-distance routes;
- later-game exploration with strong visual landmarks.

### Surface Region 8 — late-game dry/volcanic frontier

Broad progression emphasis: Tier VI-VIII overlap.

Functions:

- dry basins, volcanic or geologically unstable zones, salt/ash country, fortified ruins and newer settlements;
- valuable resources draw people into dangerous territory;
- large areas where old route maintenance finally broke down completely;
- late-game bosses and endgame access routes without making every square Tier VIII.

## Outer Isles

`Outer Isles` is a planning label, not necessarily the final collective name.

The Isles are deliberately mixed in difficulty rather than forming one linear Tier band.

Purposes:

- compact self-contained exploration stories;
- maritime routes and ship/ferry progression;
- secluded communities and ecological variants;
- optional bosses and secrets;
- a clean way to add later content without distorting the main landmass;
- some islands can become endgame targets while others open earlier.

## Deepways

`Deepways` is a planning label, not necessarily the final in-world name.

The underground is not one ancient civilization's perfectly unified subway.

It is a palimpsest of:

- mines;
- drainage and water works;
- service tunnels;
- fortified passages;
- natural caverns;
- older foundations;
- buried roads/structures;
- later expansions and collapses.

At first, players encounter only local pieces. Over the game they discover that some sections connect regions much farther apart than expected.

Deepways therefore functions as:

- mystery layer;
- dangerous exploration Region;
- late-game shortcut network;
- historical evidence;
- place for bosses/content that do not fit surface ecology.

## Region identity rule

No Region is designed as only a biome.

Each final Region brief must answer:

1. What terrain and climate variations exist?
2. Why do people live here?
3. What do they grow, catch, extract, make, transport, worship, defend, or trade?
4. What historical powers shaped the place?
5. What changed recently enough that living NPCs care about it?
6. What older layer is visible beneath the current culture?
7. What monsters/factions/ecological threats belong here and why?
8. What does its architecture look like and what materials are locally available?
9. What routes connect it to neighbors?
10. What can players see early that gives them a reason to return later?

## Production scope

The first vertical slice does not implement the macro world.

Initial playable target remains approximately:

- Rookvale central settlement;
- 2-3 connected field Areas;
- several Micro Sites;
- 1 Small Dungeon;
- 1 Major Dungeon;
- 1-2 Field Bosses;
- at least one visible/known location that is inaccessible or dangerously over-Tier;
- one early Deepways fragment or equivalent historical underground clue.

The slice must validate density, detours, dialogue/worldbuilding delivery, and return-interest before production expands outward.

## Naming boundary

Only `Rookvale` is currently treated as an established in-world macro-region name.

All labels such as `Outer Isles`, `Deepways`, `western march`, `highland belt`, and similar terms remain production descriptors until their cultures and naming families are researched and designed.
